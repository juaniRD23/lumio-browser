Why night skies are getting brighter

Artificial light at night is growing about 10% a year, and most of us can no longer see the Milky Way from home.

By Ana Ortiz · 9 min read

A decade of citizen-science measurements shows the night sky brightening far faster than satellite data suggested. Satellites miss much of the blue-rich light from LED street lamps, which scatters more in the atmosphere.

The effects go beyond astronomy. Migrating birds lose their way over lit cities, insects gather at lamps instead of pollinating, and people sleep worse when bedrooms never get fully dark.

The fixes are simple and cheap: shield lights so they point down, use warmer color temperatures, and dim or switch off lights late at night. Towns that did this saved money on energy and got their stars back.
